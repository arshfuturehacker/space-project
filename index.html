Orbital mechanics

Gravity is the guide.

Orbit is not “floating.” An orbiting body is continually falling toward a larger body while its sideways velocity carries it forward. The balance creates a curved path.

Light-years

Distance can be time.

A light-year is a distance, not a duration: the distance light travels in one year. Looking farther into space means looking farther back into cosmic history.

Scale

The universe resists intuition.

Planetary distances are already enormous. Stellar and galactic scales stretch that intuition until familiar units stop being useful.

The ISS travels in low Earth orbit. Its position is constantly changing because orbital motion is continuous, not a sequence of static locations.

Lagrange points

Useful places in a moving system.

In a two-body system, certain regions let spacecraft maintain useful relative geometry with less station-keeping effort. The Sun–Earth L1 point is one example.

Human spaceflight

Telemetry

01 · Planetary baseline

A half-scale desert with real weather

Mars looks static from a telescope. Up close it has seasons, dust storms that swallow the globe, and swings in temperature that would shatter most equipment.

Mars orbits the Sun at 142 million miles out — 1.5 times Earth's distance — so sunlight arriving there left the Sun about 13 minutes earlier. A 25-degree axial tilt gives it four seasons of uneven length, stretched out by its more eccentric orbit around the Sun.

Surface temperature swings from a mild 70°F (20°C) at the equator in summer to -225°F (-153°C) at the poles. The atmosphere, mostly carbon dioxide with traces of nitrogen and argon, is under 1% the density of Earth's — thin enough that any human visitor needs a pressure suit, and thin enough that landing anything heavier than a golf cart requires retro-rockets rather than parachutes alone.

SCALE CHECK If Earth were a nickel, Mars would be about the size of a raspberry sitting next to it.

Global dust storms are a genuine hazard, not weather-app trivia: high winds loft fine, oxidized iron dust into the thin air, sometimes blotting out the sun for weeks across the entire planet — the same rust-colored dust that gives Mars its color and, eventually, its nickname.

"It blows my mind." — an astrobiologist, on 2026's organic-chemistry findings from Gale Crater.
02 · What the rovers are finding

The chemistry of a planet that might once have lived

Two rovers, two craters, and a run of discoveries in 2026 that keep pointing the same direction: ancient Mars had the right chemistry for life, even if life itself is still unproven.

JEZERO CRATER · PERSEVERANCE Using its SHERLOC laser instrument, Perseverance detected widespread macromolecular carbon inside mudstones at the Bright Angel formation — organic compounds locked in during two separate geological eras, first by an ancient lakebed, then by later groundwater.
CHEYAVA FALLS Millimeter-scale "leopard spot" patterns turned up in the same rock — the kind of iron-and-phosphate reaction pattern that, on Earth, is usually a byproduct of ancient microbial metabolism.
GALE CRATER · CURIOSITY Curiosity identified 21 distinct carbon-based molecules in a 2020 rock sample, including several never seen on Mars before — among them a nitrogen-ring structure that's a chemical predecessor to RNA and DNA.
ACCIDENTAL FIND The rover cracked a rock open by accident while driving over it and exposed yellow crystals of pure elemental sulfur — a first for Mars, and a total surprise to the mission team.

Curiosity logged its 5,000th sol on Mars in late 2026, still working through the layered lakebed strata of Mount Sharp.

07 · What's next

The 2026–2030s roadmap

Five space agencies, one destination. Here's who's launching what, and when.

2026
Curiosity hits its 5,000th sol. JAXA's MMX launches to retrieve a Phobos sample. NASA's twin ESCAPADE orbiters launch to study atmospheric stripping.
2027
ESCAPADE arrives at Mars to begin studying how solar wind strips away the atmosphere.
2028
China's Tianwen-3 launches on a dual Long March 5, targeting 500g of returned sample. ESA/NASA's Rosalind Franklin rover lands at Oxia Planum to drill 2 meters down.
2030
ISRO's Mangalyaan-2 attempts India's first Mars soft landing, with an orbiter, lander, and rover package.
2031
Tianwen-3's samples are due back on Earth — potentially the first Mars samples returned by any nation.
2030s+
SpaceX targets uncrewed Starship landings at Arcadia Planitia, with humanoid robots verifying subsurface ice before any crew follows.
